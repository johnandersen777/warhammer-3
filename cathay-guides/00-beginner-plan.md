# Grand Cathay Beginner Plan

Welcome to Grand Cathay. You are an ancient empire of dragons, magic, and a massive wall, surrounded by enemies. Your job is to hold your ground, let enemies die charging into your guns and crossbows, and keep your two big meters balanced. This plan is written for a brand-new player in the current version of the game (patch 8.0+), and notes where older advice no longer applies.

## Quick Start (first ~10 turns)

1. **Turn 1, send a caravan.** Open the Ivory Road and dispatch your first trade caravan west toward the Old World — pick a Caravan Master, assign cargo, choose a destination. Check the route and risks on the Ivory Road map first. This is your main money engine; it offsets the drain of building armies.
2. **Point the Wu-Xing Compass at Growth.** Set it and check it each turn. Growth early speeds up building out your settlements and the Great Bastion gates.
3. **Build up the Great Bastion gate garrisons right away.** The Kurgan raiders from the north will start poking your wall. Garrison buildings mean the gates hold themselves and you don't have to park an army there. The gates eventually pay for themselves — one of their building chains reduces upkeep for garrisoned armies by up to 90%.
4. **Balance Harmony from day one.** Buildings, technology, and some lords are Yin or Yang. Try to alternate so the meter sits near the middle. Keep one empty building slot so you can demolish/rebuild to rebalance, and remember recruiting then disbanding a cheap hero nudges the meter too. The balanced bonus is worth a lot; a tilted meter is a penalty.
5. **Unite Cathay first, above everything.** There are hostile rebel factions in your starting region and loyalist ones you can confederate. Beat the rebels and absorb the loyalists as fast as you can — their provinces are a huge income jump. Cathay earns like High Elves, so more territory equals a lot more money.
6. **Pick your starter army around missiles.** For Miao Ying (the recommended beginner lord), that's Jade Warrior Crossbowmen and Grand Cannons. Early enemies (Chaos, Norsca, Ogres) have poor armor, so your crossbows shred them.
7. **Take Jade Warrior Crossbowmen with Shields**, not the plain ones — the shields let them tank enemy fire much better.
8. **In 8.0, pick your tech branch immediately.** The tech tree is now two branches: Military (units and combat buffs) or Provinces (buildings, taxes, income, diplomacy). Decide on turn one whether you're investing in war or economy and commit — don't skip around.
9. **Push a lord to rank 12.** That unlocks the free legendary hero Taoyan the Merciless through a mission, for any Cathay lord. No DLC needed — don't miss a free hero.
10. **Don't overextend.** Hold your home provinces, let the Bastion handle the north, and expand slowly. Cathay is a defensive empire — secure home ground before pushing out.

## Core Mechanics Explained Simply

- **Harmony (Yin / Yang):** Your faction identity. Every unit, building, and tech is Yin or Yang — Yin is mostly ranged, Yang is mostly melee. On the campaign map, keeping your Yin and Yang choices balanced gives bonuses; drifting one way gives penalties. In battle, a Yin and Yang unit fighting next to each other buff each other — your shooters reload faster, your fighters get better melee defense. It applies to everything you do, so balance is your core skill.
- **The Great Bastion:** Your northern wall, attacked by Chaos/Kurgan raiders from turn one. Build its garrison buildings so the gates defend themselves. Protecting it protects your whole empire, and the right buildings give big campaign bonuses.
- **Ivory Road caravans:** Send trade caravans west to earn money. Each run hits random events — some boost the reward, a bad dilemma choice can destroy the caravan. Send them early and keep them running; this is your main income source and also opens diplomacy with the Old World.
- **Wu-Xing Compass:** A magic compass that points one of four ways for a faction-wide bonus — money, growth, military, or control. Point it at whatever you're struggling with this turn, and leave it pointed the same way long enough to fill its meter for a bigger payoff.

## Your Army

**Early-game template (roughly what you want by turn 10-15):**
- **Front line:** Peasant Long Spearmen as cheap screen, then Jade Warrior Halberds as your real line-holders. Halberds beat the sword-and-board variant — more armor-piercing damage and better against monsters.
- **Behind them, in Harmony range:** Jade Warrior Crossbowmen (with Shields) — your main damage dealers early.
- **Artillery:** 1-2 Grand Cannons. The most accurate cannons in the game, and they can even shoot flying monsters out of the sky.
- **Keep it a tight turtle.** Cathay units are weaker than other factions' equivalents when fighting alone — the Harmony buff from standing next to an opposite-type unit is what makes them work. A lone unit is a mistake.

**What to upgrade into:**
- Swap Peasants for **Celestial Dragon Guard** (top-tier infantry) and **Celestial Dragon Crossbowmen** as income allows.
- Add **Crane Gunners** — long-range armor-piercing snipers that pick off lords and monsters.
- Add **Terracotta Sentinels** — huge unbreakable constructs, arguably the best monster in the game. They anchor your line and don't even need Harmony.
- **Bring 2+ spellcasters in every army.** Every caster past the first boosts the power of all your spells by 15%, stacking to +100%. This is a bigger deal for Cathay than any other faction — it's worth hiring an extra wizard even if they rarely cast. An Astromancer and an Alchemist as heroes are your go-tos.
- Leave Jade Warriors and their crossbowmen **standing still** — they get bonuses (armor and charge resistance) for not moving. Plant them and let the enemy come to you.
- Do **not** build for cavalry. Cathay has the worst cavalry in the game. You win with guns, artillery, and monsters, not charges.

## Buildings & Economy

In your first settlement, roughly this order:

1. **Growth building first** so your settlements level faster (and pair it with the Compass on Growth early).
2. **A Bastion gate garrison building** on the wall — the single most important early investment.
3. **Balance buildings:** build the first tier of a Yin building and a Yang building to get both balance points. You only need the first tier for the balance bonus — don't overbuild.
4. **Income buildings** (tax/industry) once the growth ball is rolling.
5. **A tier-3 defense building in every province** — this stops the Chaos Rifts from spawning in your territory in the Realms of Chaos campaign and saves you a fortune in cleanup.
6. **At the Bastion gates, the upkeep-reduction chain** — up to 90% cheaper garrisoned armies, so your northern defense basically runs itself.
7. **In 8.0, tech into the Provinces branch** for tax, income, and diplomacy bonuses on the economy side.

Your economy runs on the **Ivory Road** (caravans), **confederating Cathay provinces** (your single biggest income jump), and **keeping Harmony balanced**. Don't neglect any of the three.

## Common Newbie Mistakes

- **Letting your Yin and Yang units fight apart** — a split army is an un-buffed, weak army. Keep your turtle tight.
- **...but packing too tight** — clustering makes you a magnet for enemy vortex and wind spells. Keep your blocks close enough for Harmony, far enough apart that one spell doesn't hit everything.
- **Moving your Jade Warriors and crossbowmen** — they get armor and charge resistance for standing still. Let the enemy advance into you.
- **Tilting the campaign Harmony meter** — you trade your faction-wide bonuses for penalties. Alternate Yin/Yang picks and keep a free building slot to rebalance.
- **Leaving your wizards in melee** — they are exceptionally fragile. Keep lords and heroes behind the line.
- **Ignoring the Great Bastion** — the north will bleed you if the gates can't hold.
- **Sending a caravan without reading the dilemma** — a bad choice can destroy the caravan and your income with it.
- **Keeping Peasant units too long** — they're cheap fodder, not a line. Replace them with Jade Warriors and Celestial Guard as fast as you can afford it.
- **Fighting a cavalry or skirmish game** — you can't win it. Don't try to chase routers or hunt artillery with horsemen.
- **Using Sky Lanterns expecting damage** — they're support/scouting at best (and in 8.0, see below, their job changed again).

## Patch Note

**What 8.0 changed that older guides get wrong (guides written before the 8.0 rework describe an older Cathay):**

- **The tech tree was split in two.** Older guides treat it as one big tree. Now it's two branches — **Military** (units, combat buffs) and **Provinces** (buildings, taxes, income, diplomacy). You choose your direction from turn one, and each research now affects a wider set of things.
- **Harmony is no longer the single empire-wide meter older guides describe.** Older guides tell you to balance one meter across your whole empire; the 8.0 rework restructured how it's applied. Think in terms of keeping each province/region balanced rather than chasing one global meter.
- **Sky Lanterns changed role.** Older guides call them long-range snipers. In 8.0 they are infantry disruption units — fly them into enemy formations to break them up, don't expect them to snipe single targets.
- **New units and lord:** the 8.0 lord **Bhashiva** plays ambush and harassment with her exclusive **Tiger Warriors** (plus Stalker and Iron Claw variants) — the opposite of Miao Ying's defensive turtle. Don't try to play her like the old lords.
- **Tiger Warriors were hotfixed:** 8.0.2 (June 2026) raised their recruitment cost — budget for Bhashiva's armies accordingly.
- **Free hero:** **Taoyan the Merciless** is a free legendary hero for every Cathay lord via a mission at lord rank 12. No older guide will tell you about him because he didn't exist.
- **Most core advice still holds:** the Ivory Road caravans, the Wu-Xing Compass, the Great Bastion garrisons, Battle Harmony (melee + ranged buffing each other), and bringing 2+ casters per army are all unchanged and still the heart of how Cathay plays.

**Recommended starter:** Play **Miao Ying**. She's the defensive, missile-and-artillery-focused lord — exactly the playstyle that makes Cathay forgiving for a new player: hold ground, balance your meters, send caravans, and let the enemy die on your wall of guns.
