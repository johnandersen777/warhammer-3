Total War: Warhammer III · Grand Cathay · Beginner Primer

Hold the wall.
Balance the scales.
Let them die on your guns.

Grand Cathay is an empire of dragons, magic, and one very big wall. You win by standing still — shooters behind a line of spears, two meters kept in balance, and a caravan of gold rolling west every turn.

Recommended starter: Miao Ying, the Storm Dragon — defensive, missile-heavy, forgiving. Press → or Begin to start.

Your first ten turns

Tap each one off as you do it. Progress saves in your browser.

The four mechanics, plainly

Yin / Yang

Harmony

Every unit, building and tech is Yin (mostly ranged) or Yang (mostly melee). Keep them balanced on the campaign map for big bonuses; in battle, a Yin and Yang unit next to each other buff each other. Balance is the whole skill.

Defense

The Great Bastion

Your northern wall, hit by Chaos raiders from turn one. Build its garrisons and the gates hold themselves — no army needed. Its upkeep chain can cut garrisoned-army costs by up to 90%.

Income

Ivory Road caravans

Send trade caravans west to earn money — your main income engine. Each run rolls random events; a bad dilemma choice can destroy the caravan. Send early, keep them moving.

Tool

Wu-Xing Compass

A magic compass pointing one of four ways for a faction-wide bonus: money, growth, military, or control. Point it at whatever you need this turn, and hold the direction to fill its meter for a bigger payoff.

Your army is a turtle

Cathay units are weak alone — the Harmony buff from a Yin unit standing beside a Yang unit is what makes them work. Plant the line and let the enemy come to you.

Upgrade into

Celestial Dragon Guard + Celestial Dragon Crossbowmen, Crane Gunners, Terracotta Sentinels.

Always bring

2+ spellcasters — each extra caster boosts all your spells +15%, stacking to +100%.

Never build

Cavalry. Cathay has the worst cavalry in the game. Guns, artillery and monsters win.

What to build first

  1. Growth building first, and point the Compass at Growth — level up faster.
  2. Bastion gate garrison on the wall — the single most important early investment.
  3. Balance buildings: first tier of one Yin and one Yang building. First tier only — you get the balance point without overbuilding.
  4. Income buildings (tax / industry) once growth is rolling.
  5. Tier-3 defense building in every province — stops Chaos Rifts from spawning in your land.
  6. At the gates, the upkeep-reduction chain — your northern defense runs itself.
  7. In 8.0, tech into the Provinces branch for tax, income and diplomacy.

Economy = caravans + confederating Cathay provinces + balanced Harmony. Don't neglect any of the three.

Population & growth

"Growth" is your surplus population filling toward the next settlement tier. More tier = better buildings, better recruits, and Terracotta Sentinels at gate level 4.

Biggest lever

Wu-Xing Compass → Growth

Point it at Growth early and check every turn. Leave it there to fill the meter for a bigger payoff. The single best early growth source, especially for Bastion gates.

Passive

Balanced Harmony

A balanced Yin/Yang meter grants a faction-wide Growth bonus (plus income, control, relations). Tilt it and you lose it.

Building

Growth building first

Build the settlement growth building first, then pair it with the Compass on Growth. First tier is enough — don't overbuild.

Watch

Climate

Green climate grows fast; yellow/red climates grow slowly and need the Yin public-order building for replenishment. Prefer green provinces for expansion.

Lore note: Cathay has the largest human population — reflected in cheap, numerous Peasant Spearmen and Archers. Spend that bodies-to-spare edge early.

Don't make these mistakes

⚠Letting Yin and Yang fight apart — a split army is an un-buffed, weak army. Keep the turtle tight.
⚠…but packing too tight — clustering invites enemy vortex and wind spells. Close for Harmony, spread enough that one spell doesn't hit everything.
⚠Moving your Jade units — they get armor and charge resistance for standing still. Let the enemy advance into you.
⚠Tilting the Harmony meter — trade faction bonuses for penalties. Alternate Yin/Yang picks, keep a free slot to rebalance.
⚠Sending a caravan without reading the dilemma — one bad choice destroys the caravan and your income.
⚠Keeping Peasants too long — they're fodder, not a line. Swap to Jade Warriors and Celestial Guard as soon as you can afford it.

What patch 8.0 changed

Most guides were written before the 8.0 rework. Here's what's different now.

Changed

Tech tree split

Now two branches: Military (units, combat) and Provinces (buildings, taxes, income). Pick your direction on turn one and commit.

Changed

Harmony is local

No longer one empire-wide meter. Balance each province/region instead of chasing one global number.

Changed

Sky Lanterns

No longer snipers — they're now infantry-disruption units. Fly them into formations to break them up.

New

Bhashiva & Taoyan

New lord Bhashiva plays ambush/harassment with exclusive Tiger Warriors. Free hero Taoyan the Merciless unlocks for any lord at rank 12.

Everything else holds: caravans, the Compass, Bastion garrisons, battle Harmony, and 2+ casters per army.

Unite Cathay first

Your single biggest income jump. Do it in this order.

  1. Kill the four rebel factions — Cathay Rebels, Rebel Lords of Nan-Yang, Dissenter Lords of Jinshen, Burning Wind Nomads (two per lord).
  2. Confederate the loyalists — Celestial Loyalists + Imperial Wardens, easy once the rebels are beaten.
  3. Kill Azazel ASAP — new threat by the Bastion; ignored he spams stacks at you. Grand Cannons snipe him out of the air.
  4. Stay out of the Mountains of Mourn early — full of Ogres + Chaos Warriors. Late-game only.
  5. Skip the Steppes beyond the Bastion — let the gate garrisons hold the north.
  6. Zhao Ming tip: Miao Ying's AI holds the Bastion for you while you conquer/confederate all Cathay — easiest path.

Build better units

Three-tier ladder: Peasant → Jade Warrior → Celestial Dragon. Your economy decides how fast you climb.

Infantry

Peasant Long Spearmen → Jade Warriors (halberd = bread & butter) → Celestial Dragon Guard (elite AP). Jade stays cost-effective all game.

Missiles

Peasant Archers → Jade Crossbowmen → Celestial Dragon Crossbowmen (shielded, AP). Miao Ying's −50% upkeep makes full stacks her endgame.

Specialists

Iron Hail Gunners (AP shotgun) → replaced by Crane Gunners (long-range snipers) ASAP, 1–2 per army.

Artillery

Grand Cannon = main damage (accurate, flaming). Fire Rain Rocket = anti-blob, not armored.

Monster

Terracotta Sentinel — only monster, best unit, unbreakable. Doomstack material, especially Zhao Ming's −25% Yang upkeep.

Skip

Cavalry — worst in the game. Flying Sky Lantern / Sky-junk are fragile support; protect them with Longma Riders.

Army templates by era

Every army runs 2+ casters (each past the first = +15% spell power, to +100%). No cavalry.

  1. Early (turns 10–15): 1 Lord, 2–3 Peasant Spears, 3–4 Jade Halberds, 4–6 Jade Crossbows (shields), 1–2 Grand Cannons, + Astromancer/Alchemist.
  2. Mid: swap Peasants → Celestial Dragon Guard + Celestial Crossbowmen, add 1–2 Crane Gunners + 1–2 Terracotta Sentinels.
  3. Miao Ying late (missile stack): Miao, ~12–14 Celestial Dragon Crossbowmen, 4–6 Sentinels, 2 casters, 2 Grand Cannons.
  4. Zhao Ming late (Yang stack): Zhao, Alchemist + Astromancer, 1–2 Longma/Sky-junks, rest Terracotta Sentinels.
  5. Generic late core: 6–8 Celestial Crossbows + 2 Crane Gunners + 4–6 Sentinels + halberds + 2 casters.

The Great Bastion

Your northern wall vs Chaos. Three gates: Snake (Miao Ying's), Dragon, Turtle.

Threat meter

Builds every turn; spikes if a gate falls or Chaos masses outside; max = major invasion. Hold the gates so it never fires at a bad time.

Kurgan raids

Norscan-style warbands with no real siege (trolls/mammoths only) — they mostly die against your garrisons for free XP.

Upkeep chain

Build it at every gate — up to 90% upkeep reduction for garrisoned armies. "Gates practically pay for themselves."

Level 4

Get each gate to level 4 to build Terracotta Sentinels there.

Fund it with Ivory Road caravans + point the Compass at Growth to speed gate construction.

Retaking the Dragon Gate

A fallen gate spikes the Bastion Threat meter — non-Cathay ownership is a big penalty. Take it back, then rebuild it to hold itself.

Why it matters

A gate lost to Chaos/rebels accelerates the Threat meter toward a major invasion. Gates are special 4-slot settlements — garrison, supplies, ammo, and reduced army upkeep.

It's a siege

The gate is a walled settlement. Breach it, then assault — you can't just walk in.

  1. Bring artillery to the siege — 2+ Grand Cannons to breach walls/towers before you commit troops.
  2. Siege and assault — breach, then send Jade Halberds through the gap; clear the garrison and capture.
  3. Repair the damaged buildings first (cheap, fast).
  4. Build the garrison building so the gate defends itself again.
  5. Build the upkeep-reduction chain — up to 90% cheaper garrisoned armies.
  6. Level it to 4 to unlock Terracotta Sentinels (tier 5 also trims the Wu-Xing Compass cooldown).
  7. Rebalance Harmony if the gate's buildings are Yin/Yang — a free slot makes it easy.

Recapturing drops Threat (each destroyed enemy army −20%); holding it with a built garrison keeps the meter from spiking again.

Ivory Road caravans

Main income engine, available turn 1. Auto-travels west, rolling random events.

Setup

Pick a Caravan Master, assign cargo, pick a destination — the map previews route + risks. Reaches Kislev's Ice Court and the Old World.

Risks

Never route through hostile territory; don't dispatch during the 15-turn Chaos Rift windows (daemonic incursions).

Unique items

Von Carstein Blade (regeneration — ideal on Miao Ying), Luminark Lens (40% fire weakness map-wide), Catalytic Kiln (mortis aura).

Diplomacy

Routes unlock trade treaties → military treaties → Outposts (allied unit recruitment).

Destinations + rewards: Altdorf (Luminark Lens), Castle Drakenhof (Von Carstein Blade), Erengrad (Gryphon Legion Lance), Karaz-a-Karak (The Ankor Chain).

Harmony, in depth

Yin (ranged) vs Yang (melee). Balanced = bonuses; tilted = penalties.

Campaign

Buildings, techs, lords (+3), heroes (+1) each carry a Yin/Yang cost. Drift too far = inefficiency and provincial instability.

Battle

Yang melee get leadership + melee defense near Yin; Yin ranged get leadership + reload near Yang. Packed turtle → watch for AoE spells.

Rebalance cheaply

Recruit/disband a hero (+1 each), keep a free building slot, build only tier 1 for the point, demolish what you don't need.

Patch note

Patch 4.0 reportedly made Harmony province-local (no top-screen meter) — but older guides still describe it empire-wide. Verify in-game.

Defending the west — Khorne

Khorne is melee-only: no ranged, no casters, just a wall of frenzied warriors and daemons. Your whole plan is shoot them down before they reach you, then hold with armor-piercing halberds.

Threat

What Khorne does

Chaos Warriors (heavy armor), Bloodletters & Bloodthirsters (daemons), Skullcrushers (fast heavy cavalry). Frenzy + huge charge = they win open melee every time.

Opening

What Khorne can't do

Zero ranged, zero spellcasters, and magic resistance. They must walk into your guns. Exploit it — kill everything before contact.

Counters

AP + anti-large: Jade Halberds, Celestial Dragon Guard halberds, Crane Gunners, Iron Hail Gunners, Grand Cannons, Terracotta Sentinel.

Won't work

Cavalry, Fire Rain Rockets vs armor, Peasant Archers, and wasting magic winds on magic-resistant daemons.
